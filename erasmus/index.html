Curriculum Structure 5 Core Pillars

Class 4B INF: Telecommunications Pillars

The 4th year is the pivotal transition where theoretical physics and mathematical analysis converge into real-world communication hardware, high-frequency lines, and protocols.

01
Signal Theory

Deterministic vs stochastic signals. Continuous, sampled, and digital waveforms. Peak, RMS, average parameters with live oscilloscope controls.

02
Fourier Spectral Analysis

Fourier series decomposition of periodic composite waves into sinusoidal harmonics. Harmonic spectrum, bandwidth, and live waveform synthesis.

03
Transmission Media

Guided media (twisted pair, coax, Single-Mode and Multi-Mode optical fibers) with interactive Snell's Law and total internal reflection simulator.

04
Modulation Systems

Analog (AM, FM) and digital shift keying (ASK, FSK) with real-time waveform modulation sliders and Nyquist-Shannon sampling visualization.

05
Protocols & Architecture

OSI 7-layer and TCP/IP models, Ethernet framing, VLANs, and digital oscilloscope "Rule of 5" bandwidth calibration simulator.

🔬
Hands-on Laboratory

40% of class time is spent on bench testing with digital storage oscilloscopes (DSOs), arbitrary waveform generators, and Cisco Packet Tracer.

From Galileo's Pulse to AC Electricity

In 1581, a young Galileo Galilei watched a swinging chandelier in the Cathedral of Pisa, timing its rhythm with his own pulse and discovering that harmonic oscillations maintain a constant period regardless of amplitude!

$$\displaystyle s(t) = A_0 \cos(2\pi f_0 t) + V_{offset}$$
  • Peak $V_p$ & Span $V_{pp}$: How far the pendulum swings from center and its total crest-to-trough travel.
  • RMS ($V_{rms}$): The real "muscle" of the wave—the equivalent DC battery voltage delivering identical thermal heating work ($V_{p} / \sqrt{2}$).

Napoleon's Cannons & The Musical Choir

In 1807, Jean-Baptiste Fourier was studying heat diffusion in Napoleon's brass cannon barrels when he made a stunning claim: any arbitrary periodic wave—even one with jagged vertical corners—can be assembled purely from smooth musical sine waves!

$$\displaystyle \text{Square}(t) = \frac{4}{\pi} \left( \sin(\omega t) + \frac{1}{3}\sin(3\omega t) + \frac{1}{5}\sin(5\omega t) + \dots \right)$$

Academic titans—including our school's namesake Joseph-Louis Lagrange—initially rejected Fourier's paper, arguing smooth curves could never form sharp right angles. But Fourier was right: add enough odd harmonics, and square digital pulses emerge!

Tyndall's Light Water Jet & Skipping Stones

Global subsea fiber optics carry over 95% of world internet data, but the physics began as a Victorian public spectacle! In 1854, John Tyndall shone an arc lamp into a draining water barrel in London, proving light could be trapped inside a curving water stream.

$$\displaystyle \text{Light Trapping Criterion:} \quad \theta \ge \theta_c = \arcsin\left(\frac{n_{\text{cladding}}}{n_{\text{core}}}\right)$$

The Skipping Stone Analogy: Throw a stone steeply into water, and it plunges under (optical loss into cladding). Skip it at a shallow grazing angle ($\theta \ge \theta_c$), and 100% bounces cleanly across the lake without losing a single photon!

Marconi's Spark & The 75-km Antenna Dilemma

Why can't we transmit human voice directly through the air? Physics dictates antenna size must match wavelength ($\ell \approx \lambda / 4$). Transmitting an audible 1 kHz note would require an antenna 75 kilometers tall—nearly nine times higher than Mount Everest!

$$\displaystyle \ell \approx \frac{c}{4 f_c} \quad \implies \text{At } 100\,\text{MHz (FM)}, \quad \ell = 75\,\text{cm!}$$

The Passenger Airplane Metaphor: Our baseband voice cannot fly alone. We board it onto a high-frequency radio carrier wave ($f_c$). In AM, voice alters the wave's altitude; in FM, it alters its stride rhythm (Carson's rule: $B \approx 2(\Delta f + f_m)$), conferring immunity against thunderstorm static!

Bandwidth formula: B = 2 · f_m

Braun's CRT & The High-Speed Camera Rule

When Karl Ferdinand Braun invented the cathode ray tube in 1897, oscilloscopes tracked slow physical heartbeats. But in digital computing, clock pulses jump between 0 and 1 in nanoseconds. How fast must an oscilloscope be to see reality?

Bscope ≥ 5 × fsignal   |   trise ≈ 0.35 / Bscope

The Blurry Shutter Dilemma: If an oscilloscope's bandwidth is too narrow, it acts like a slow camera shutter trying to photograph a hummingbird's wings. High Fourier harmonics are stripped away, turning crisp square clock edges into rounded, sleepy sine waves!

Graham Bell's Ear & Laboratory Practice

Why do telecom engineers speak in Decibels (dB)? In the 1920s, Bell System engineers honored Alexander Graham Bell by creating a logarithmic scale matching human hearing. Whispers and thunderclaps span a factor of 100,000,000! Decibels turn tedious decimal multiplications across kilometers of cable into simple addition and subtraction ($P_{out} = P_{in} - \text{Loss}$).

  • Inquiry benches: Pairs of students measure physical fiber loss with optical power meters.
  • Simulation first: Virtual modeling in LTspice and Cisco Packet Tracer before hardware.
  • Engineering report: Rigorous calculation of margin budgets and experimental error.
