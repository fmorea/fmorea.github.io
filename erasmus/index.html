Syllabus Deep-Dive Module 1

Signal Classification & Time-Domain Parameters

Establishing the formal physical and mathematical properties of telecommunication waveforms before circuit transmission.

📈
Deterministic vs. Stochastic Signals

Deterministic Signals: Exactly predictable by closed-form math: $$\displaystyle s(t) = A_0 \cos(2\pi f_0 t + \varphi_0)$$ Reproducible in labs using signal generators; ideal for bench calibration and frequency response measurements.

Random / Aleatory Signals: Non-deterministic instantaneous values (e.g. human speech, thermal noise $P_n = kTB$). Characterized via probability distributions, power spectral density, and variance.

⚡
Waveform Categories
  • Analog Signals: Continuous in time, continuous in amplitude (e.g. raw microphone output).
  • Sampled Signals: Discrete in time (at sampling intervals $T_s$), continuous in voltage.
  • Digital Signals: Discrete in both time and quantized discrete amplitude levels (binary bits $0$ and $1$).
Peak & Period

Peak voltage $V_p$, Peak-to-Peak $V_{pp} = 2V_p$, and Period $T$ (seconds) where fundamental frequency is $f_0 = 1/T$ (Hz).

Average Value (DC)

Arithmetic mean over period $T$: $$\displaystyle V_{avg} = \frac{1}{T}\int_0^T s(t)\,dt$$ Equals $0\,\text{V}$ for symmetrical AC waveforms.

RMS Effective Value

Equivalent thermal power dissipation: $$\displaystyle V_{rms} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T s^2(t)\,dt} = \frac{V_p}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.707 V_p$$

Syllabus Deep-Dive Live Interactive Demo

Fourier Spectral Synthesis in Real-Time

Jean-Baptiste Fourier's theorem states that any periodic wave can be reconstructed as a sum of sinusoidal harmonics. Test it live below!

𝝨
Fourier Series Equation

$$\displaystyle s(t) = a_0 + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left[ a_n \cos(n \omega_0 t) + b_n \sin(n \omega_0 t) \right]$$

  • Fundamental: $f_0 = 1/T$ (first harmonic, determines pitch).
  • Harmonics: Integer multiples $f_n = n \cdot f_0$.
  • Bandwidth $B$: Transmission channel must transmit sufficient harmonics to prevent severe edge rounding.

💡 Didactic observation for Erasmus teachers: Notice how odd harmonics ($1f, 3f, 5f\dots$) assemble with amplitude proportional to $1/n$ to sharpen the rise-time edges of the square wave, demonstrating why digital lines require broadband frequency response.

Syllabus Deep-Dive Module 3

Transmission Media & Optical Physics

Exploring guided copper media, optical waveguide physics, attenuation curves, and high-frequency dispersion phenomena.

➿
Twisted Pair (Cat 6/6A)

Differential balanced transmission canceling electromagnetic interference (crosstalk). Characteristic impedance $Z_0 \approx 100\,\Omega$. Limited by attenuation to 100 meters per IEEE standard.

🔘
Coaxial Cables

Concentric cylindrical shield offering high EMI immunity up to GHz range. $50\,\Omega$ impedance for RF transceivers; $75\,\Omega$ optimized for minimum video attenuation.

✨
Single-Mode Fiber (SMF)

Narrow core ($9\,\mu\text{m}$), eliminating intermodal dispersion. Laser excitation at $1310\,\text{nm}$ and $1550\,\text{nm}$. Enables 100+ km backbone transmission links.

📐
Optical Waveguide Physics

Based on Total Internal Reflection (Snell's Law): $$\displaystyle n_{core} > n_{cladding} \implies \theta_c = \arcsin\left(\frac{n_{cladding}}{n_{core}}\right)$$ Light rays arriving at angles greater than critical angle $\theta_c$ are trapped within the glass core without leakage.

📉
Attenuation Mechanisms

Optical loss measured in $\text{dB/km}$:

  • Rayleigh Scattering: dominant at lower wavelengths ($\propto 1/\lambda^4$).
  • Infrared Absorption: dominant above $1600\,\text{nm}$.
  • $1550\,\text{nm}$ Window: global attenuation minimum (~$0.2\,\text{dB/km}$).
Syllabus Deep-Dive Module 4

Modulation Systems & Nyquist-Shannon Sampling

Why we modulate high-frequency carrier waves, analog AM/FM schemes, digital constellations, and the mathematical criteria for sampling.

📡
Why Modulation is Mandatory

1. Antenna Sizing constraint: Optimal dipole size $\ell \approx \lambda/2 = c / (2f)$. At $1\,\text{kHz}$, $\ell = 150\,\text{km}$! Modulating at $2.4\,\text{GHz}$ shrinks the antenna to $6.25\,\text{cm}$.

2. Frequency Division Multiplexing (FDM): Enables thousands of separate communication channels across the shared electromagnetic spectrum.

⏱️
Nyquist-Shannon Sampling Theorem

To reconstruct a continuous analog signal of bandwidth $f_{max}$ without aliasing distortion, the sampling rate $f_s$ must satisfy: $$\displaystyle f_s \ge 2 f_{max}$$ In lab practice, telephony samples voice ($f_{max} = 3.4\,\text{kHz}$) at $8\,\text{kHz}$, and CD audio ($20\,\text{kHz}$) at $44.1\,\text{kHz}$.

Analog Modulation

AM: carrier amplitude modulated by information ($B = 2f_m$).
FM: Carson's rule $B \approx 2(\Delta f + f_m)$, superior SNR and noise resilience.

Binary Digital Shift Keying

ASK: Amplitude Shift Keying.
FSK: Frequency Shift Keying.
BPSK: Phase Shift Keying ($0^\circ / 180^\circ$, 1 bit/symbol).

M-ary QAM Constellations

Quadrature Amplitude Modulation: combining phase & amplitude.
Bit rate: $R_b = R_s \cdot \log_2(M)$. 64-QAM delivers 6 bits per transmitted symbol!

Syllabus Deep-Dive Module 5

Network Protocols & Measurement Instrumentation

Interconnecting the physical signal layer with Ethernet architectures, TCP/IP networking, and digital oscilloscope calibration criteria.

🌐
Layered Protocol Architecture

OSI 7-Layer vs. TCP/IP 4-Layer:

  • Physical (L1): Bit transmission, encoding (Manchester, 4B5B, PAM-4).
  • Data Link (L2): IEEE 802.3 Ethernet framing, 48-bit MAC addressing, CSMA/CD, VLAN tagging (802.1Q).
  • Network (L3): IPv4/IPv6 packet routing, subnetting (CIDR), ICMP.
  • Transport (L4): TCP reliable streams vs. UDP low-latency datagrams.
🎯
Digital Oscilloscope "Rule of 5"

To measure high-speed digital clocks without distorting rise time $t_r$ and harmonics:

Bscope ≥ 5 × Bsignal

Captures up to the 5th harmonic with <3% amplitude attenuation error. Scope rise time is governed by $t_r \approx 0.35 / B_{scope}$.

Sampling Rate Criterion

Real-time DSO sampling rate must be $f_{sample} \ge 4 \text{ to } 10 \times B_{scope}$ to accurately reconstruct fast transient spikes.

Probe Compensation

$10\times$ passive probes reduce circuit capacitive loading by a factor of 10, requiring periodic trimmer capacitor calibration.

Spectrum Analyzers

Hardware FFT analyzers transform time-domain traces into RF power spectral density plots in $\text{dBm}$ across frequency bins.

Pedagogical Framework Learning by Doing

"Didattica Laboratoriale" - Hands-on Pedagogy

Theory is proven in practice: how students in 4B INF apply theoretical equations to real electronic test benches and software emulators.

👥
Pair Benches

Students work in pairs at dedicated hardware stations equipped with digital storage oscilloscopes, function generators, multimeters, and breadboard prototypes.

💻
Hybrid Simulation

Circuit modeling in LTspice and network architecture emulation in Cisco Packet Tracer precede physical assembly, ensuring zero equipment blowouts.

📑
Technical Reports

Each experiment culminates in a formal engineering report (Relazione Tecnica) detailing circuit schematic, experimental data tables, graph plots, and error analysis.

📋
Sample 4th-Year Laboratory Practicums

1. Harmonic Verification on DSO: Students generate a $10\,\text{kHz}$ square wave, enable on-board FFT, and calculate attenuation ratios of odd harmonics to confirm Fourier theory.

2. Time-Domain Reflectometry (TDR): Injecting nanosecond pulses into coaxial lines to locate open/short faults and measure cable velocity factors.

3. Optical Power Budget: Measuring insertion loss of ST/SC connectors and fiber patch cords with calibrated optical power meters ($\text{dBm}$).

4. VLAN & Router-on-a-Stick: Configuring Cisco switches and Linux routers to segment broadcast domains and test inter-VLAN throughput.
